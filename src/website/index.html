Abstract

Current quantum processors are single, noisy QPUs with a limited qubit count. As the field moves toward utility scale computation, companies are building networks of connected QPUs, but most quantum algorithms were designed for a single machine. Distributing them requires cutting the circuit at cross QPU boundaries, and the choice of how to make that cut has a significant impact on fidelity and cost. This project benchmarks two competing distribution strategies across quantum chemistry ansätze to determine when each is preferable.

Circuit Cutting

Decomposes the circuit classically at gate boundaries. No quantum channel required, but reconstruction incurs 9k circuit evaluations per k cuts, exponential overhead that quickly becomes intractable.

Gate Teleportation

Transmits gates across QPUs using pre-shared Bell pairs as a quantum channel. Avoids exponential sampling, but fidelity decays as (1−η)n with transducer noise and gate count.

Remote Gate Fidelity vs Transducer Noise

Gate teleportation gets less accurate as the hardware connecting the two QPUs gets noisier. Current hardware sits at the right edge of this chart, meaning we need significantly cleaner connections before teleportation consistently wins.

Circuit Cutting Fidelity vs Shot Budget

Circuit cutting needs to run the experiment many times to reconstruct the result. Each additional gate cut multiplies that cost dramatically, so while it works well for simple splits, it becomes impractical quickly as circuits grow.

Roadmap

Milestone 01
Literature Review and Environment Setup
Survey of relevant literature and setup of the development environment with Qiskit and Tequila.
Milestone 02
Greedy Decision Strategy on GHZ Circuit
Implement and validate the greedy per-gate decision strategy on the GHZ circuit, reproducing the fidelity break-even result.
Milestone 03
Application to Chemistry Ansätze
Apply the strategy to UCCSD, UpCCD, UpCCGSD, and SPA+GS quantum chemistry circuits.
Milestone 04
Extension to CZ and CPhase Gates
Extend the strategy to CZ and controlled-phase gates beyond the initial CNOT implementation.
Milestone 05
Comparative Study
Full comparison across all ansätze and gate types, identifying when each strategy wins.
Milestone 06
IEEE QCE26 Workshop Paper
Write and submit a 6-page peer-reviewed paper to the IEEE QCE26 Workshop, due early July 2026.
